## Releases
[MidnightBSD 4.0.8](https://bsdsec.net/articles/midnightbsd-security-midnightbsd-4-0-8?utm_source=bsdweekly): MidnightBSD 4.0.8 was released on September 28 with updates including mport 2.8.2, expat 2.8.5, OpenSSL fixes for CVE-2026-63072 and CVE-2026-54874, and patches for multiple kernel and userland CVEs.

## BSDSec
[OpenBSD Errata: October 7, 2026 (isakmpd xserver expat wsdisplay)](https://bsdsec.net/articles/openbsd-errata-october-7-2026-isakmpd-xserver-expat-wsdisplay?utm_source=bsdweekly): OpenBSD has released errata patches for isakmpd, the X11 server, libexpat, and kernel wscons on OpenBSD 7.8 and 7.9, with binary updates available via syspatch for amd64, arm64, and i386.

As always, it's worth following [BSDSec](https://bsdsec.net). [RSS feed](https://bsdsec.net/articles.atom) available.

## News
[EuroBSDCon Trip Report – Federico Angelilli](https://freebsdfoundation.org/blog/eurobsdcon-trip-report-federico-angelilli/?utm_source=bsdweekly): A GSoC contributor describes presenting his FreeBSD kernel live-patching work at the DevSummit, attending talks on scheduler improvements and bhyve hotplugging, and meeting FreeBSD developers at EuroBSDCon 2026 in Brussels.

[EuroBSDCon 2026 Trip Report – Yan-Cheng Chen](https://freebsdfoundation.org/blog/eurobsdcon-2026-trip-report-yan-cheng-chen/?utm_source=bsdweekly): A FreeBSD Taiwan intern shares how a Foundation travel grant enabled attending EuroBSDCon, highlights from talks on enterprise FreeBSD, runtime reoptimization, and man pages, and how the event helped shape plans to contribute to FreeBSD.

[Valuable News – 2026/10/12](https://vermaden.wordpress.com/2026/10/12/valuable-news-2026-10-12/?utm_source=bsdweekly): This week's roundup covers a DTrace option for Python ports on FreeBSD, a comparison of jails vs containers, OpenSSH 10.6 and LibreSSL 4.2.2/4.3.3 releases, FreeBSD's EU Cyber Resilience Act update, NetBSD desktop and login guides, ZFS misconfigurations, and more.

[BSD Now 684: Nerds 2.0](https://www.bsdnow.tv/684?utm_source=bsdweekly): Episode 684 covers running a Telnet BBS on a Casio calculator, adding an ACPI layer to OpenBSD's vmd, building NetBSD 11 from scratch, FreeBSD backup libraries, and more.

[AsiaBSDCon 2027 - Call for papers/tutorials](https://bsdsec.net/articles/asiabsdcon-2027-tokyo-japan-march-2027-call-for-papers-tutorials-open-until-20-november-2026?utm_source=bsdweekly): The call for papers and tutorials for AsiaBSDCon 2027 in Tokyo, Japan is now open, with proposal submissions accepted through November 20, 2026.

[Tribute to Margaret Hamilton](https://www.freebsd.org/news/newsflash/#2026-10-07:1?utm_source=bsdweekly): FreeBSD would like to pay our respects to Margaret Hamilton, whose work allowed coding to become both the scientific discipline it is today, and the art form that drew each of us to it.

[Ding-Dong Ditch: The Doorbell Prank in FreeBSD's iflib](https://www.netgate.com/blog/ding-dong-ditch-the-doorbell-prank-in-freebsds-iflib?utm_source=bsdweekly): A subtle iflib bug caused redundant doorbell rings that capped a Netgate 4200 at 0.96 Mpps; a one-line fix removed the empty rings and a follow-up change batches rings per packet, taking throughput to 3.58 Mpps.

[Biggest ZFS Misconfigurations and How to Fix Them: Part 2](https://klarasystems.com/articles/zfs-misconfigurations-how-to-fix-part-2/?utm_source=bsdweekly): Covers common pitfalls with SLOG, RAIDZ expansion, Fast Dedup, Direct IO, special vdevs, and snapshots on OpenZFS 2.3+, explaining why they hurt, how to detect them, and what the fixes cost.

[New laptops and OpenBSD: HP Envy 17-DA0013dx](https://www.undeadly.org/cgi?action=article;sid=20261007053155&utm_source=bsdweekly): How to get OpenBSD to boot on an HP Envy 17-DA0013dx after initial hangs, concluding it is a viable modern laptop choice for OpenBSD with a 16:9 touchscreen.

## Tutorials
[GhostBSD: Mocka Dock | The Start of the Mocka Desktop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WM2P7o9H-G8&utm_source=bsdweekly): GhostBSD has always pushed the boundaries of what BSD and in particular FreeBSD can do, and they are still at it - the creation of the Mocka Desktop; a gradual replacement of the Mate Desktop - in this case, starting with the Mate Dock.
